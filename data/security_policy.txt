NovaTech Solutions — Information Security Policy
Classification: Internal | Version 4.1 | Effective: January 2026

============================================================
SECTION 1: PASSWORD AND AUTHENTICATION
============================================================

Password Requirements:
All passwords must meet these criteria:
- Minimum 12 characters
- At least one uppercase letter
- At least one lowercase letter
- At least one number
- At least one special character (!@#$%^&*)
- Cannot reuse the last 5 passwords
- Must be changed every 90 days

Two-Factor Authentication (2FA):
2FA is mandatory for all employees on:
- Company email
- GitHub
- AWS Console
- VPN
- HR portal (for accessing payslips and personal data)
Use Google Authenticator or Authy as your 2FA app. SMS-based 2FA is not accepted due to SIM-swap vulnerabilities.

Session Management:
- Idle sessions timeout after 30 minutes for sensitive systems (AWS, HR portal)
- General sessions (Slack, Jira) timeout after 8 hours
- Always lock your laptop when leaving your desk (Cmd+L on Mac)
- Never leave your laptop unattended in public spaces

============================================================
SECTION 2: DATA CLASSIFICATION AND HANDLING
============================================================

Data Classification Levels:
- Public: Marketing materials, blog posts, job listings
- Internal: Company policies, org charts, meeting notes
- Confidential: Customer data, financial reports, employee salaries
- Restricted: Encryption keys, database credentials, security audit findings

Handling Rules by Classification:

Public:
- Can be shared externally without approval
- No encryption required

Internal:
- Share within the company freely
- Do not post on social media or external websites
- Okay to discuss in public places (cafes, trains) at a general level

Confidential:
- Share only on a need-to-know basis within the company
- Must be encrypted when shared externally (use company-approved tools)
- Never share via personal email or WhatsApp
- Print copies must be shredded after use

Restricted:
- Access limited to named individuals only
- Must never leave company-managed systems
- Sharing requires written approval from the CISO
- Access is logged and audited quarterly

============================================================
SECTION 3: DEVICE SECURITY
============================================================

Company Laptops:
- Full disk encryption must be enabled (FileVault on Mac, BitLocker on Windows)
- Automatic OS updates must be enabled
- Company antivirus (CrowdStrike) must be installed and running
- Do not install pirated software
- Do not disable the firewall

Personal Devices:
- Personal phones can access company email and Slack ONLY if they have a screen lock (PIN/biometric)
- Do not store company files on personal devices
- If your personal device is lost or stolen, inform IT immediately

USB and External Storage:
- USB storage devices are blocked on company laptops by default
- If you need to use external storage, request a temporary exception from IT
- Never plug in USB devices found in public places

============================================================
SECTION 4: NETWORK AND REMOTE ACCESS
============================================================

Office Network:
- Connect only to the "NovaTech-Secure" WiFi network (WPA3)
- The "NovaTech-Guest" network is for visitors only — employees should not use it
- Do not connect personal IoT devices (smart speakers, etc.) to the office network

VPN:
- VPN is mandatory when working outside the office
- Use the company-provided WireGuard VPN client
- VPN connects you to the internal network — required for accessing internal tools, databases, and staging environments
- Do not use third-party VPN services for work purposes

Public WiFi:
- Avoid using public WiFi for work. Use your phone's hotspot instead
- If you must use public WiFi, ensure VPN is connected BEFORE accessing any company resources
- Never access banking, email, or sensitive systems on public WiFi without VPN

============================================================
SECTION 5: INCIDENT REPORTING
============================================================

What Counts as a Security Incident:
- Laptop lost or stolen
- Suspected unauthorized access to your accounts
- Phishing email received (even if you didn't click)
- Accidentally sharing confidential data with wrong person
- Finding confidential documents left in public areas
- Suspicious USB device found
- Any unusual account activity (login from unknown location)

How to Report:
1. Immediately inform your manager (verbal or Slack DM)
2. Email security@novatech.in with subject: "SECURITY INCIDENT — [brief description]"
3. Do NOT try to investigate on your own — let the security team handle it
4. Preserve evidence — don't delete emails, messages, or files related to the incident

Response Times:
- Critical incidents (data breach, active attack): Response within 30 minutes
- High priority (lost device, compromised account): Response within 2 hours
- Medium (phishing received, suspicious activity): Response within 24 hours
- Low (policy questions, access requests): Response within 48 hours

Phishing:
- If you receive a suspicious email, DO NOT click any links or download attachments
- Forward the email to phishing@novatech.in
- Mark it as spam/junk
- Common signs of phishing: urgency, misspelled sender domain, requests for passwords, unexpected attachments

SECTION 6: ACCEPTABLE USE
============================================================

Internet Usage:
- Company internet is primarily for work purposes
- Limited personal use is acceptable during breaks
- The following are strictly prohibited: downloading copyrighted content (movies, software), accessing explicit content, cryptocurrency mining, running personal businesses on company infrastructure

Software Installation:
- Only install software from the company-approved list (available on Confluence)
- If you need software not on the list, submit a request to IT
- Open-source tools may be used for development after checking the license (MIT, Apache OK; GPL requires IT approval)
- Never install cracked or pirated software — this is grounds for termination

AI Tools:
- ChatGPT, Claude, and similar AI tools may be used for work assistance
- NEVER paste customer data, source code, or confidential information into external AI tools
- Use the company-approved internal AI assistant for sensitive queries
- Code generated by AI must be reviewed with the same rigor as human-written code

SECTION 7: PHYSICAL SECURITY AND OFFICE ACCESS
============================================================

Badge Access:
Company offices use badge-based access control. Your badge is personal, non-transferable, and must not be shared or lent. Badge readers log entry and exit with a timestamp, and these logs are retained for 12 months.

Visitor Management:
All visitors must be registered at reception before arrival and escorted at all times. Visitors are issued a temporary badge that is valid only for the day of the visit. Visitors in restricted areas are supervised by the host at all times.

Tailgating:
Do not hold or prop open secure doors for anyone, including colleagues you recognise. If you are not sure, ask the person to badge in. Tailgating is a reportable policy violation because it defeats the access control system entirely.

Restricted Areas:
Server rooms, the finance floor, the executive floor, and the roof terrace are restricted. Access requires a documented business reason, approval from the area owner, and is revoked automatically on team change or separation. Restricted access is reviewed quarterly by facility owners.

After-Hours Access:
The office floor must be vacated by 10:00 PM on weekdays. After-hours access is granted to employees who have a business requirement and is logged for review. Working alone in the office after 8:00 PM requires check-in with a designated contact.

============================================================
SECTION 8: VENDOR AND THIRD-PARTY ACCESS
============================================================

Vendor Access Principle:
No third party receives access to NovaTech systems or data without a signed contract, a completed security review, and a named internal owner. The internal owner is accountable for the vendor's access for the duration of the relationship.

Vendor Security Review:
The review covers data the vendor handles, certifications held (SOC 2, ISO 27001), breach notification terms, sub-processor list, and data residency. Reviews take approximately 10 working days. Reviews with an expiry older than 12 months must be renewed before renewed access is granted.

Vendor Access Types:
Federated single sign-on is preferred over shared accounts. Time-boxed access is mandatory for support vendors. Support sessions must be attended by a NovaTech engineer and recorded where the vendor supports session recording.

Offboarding Vendors:
Access must be revoked within 24 hours of contract end, not within 24 hours of a reminder. Engineering must confirm in writing that all credentials, API keys, and firewall rules granted to a departing vendor have been removed.

============================================================
SECTION 9: SECURITY TRAINING AND PHISHING SIMULATION
============================================================

Mandatory Training:
All employees complete security awareness training annually. New employees complete it within the first 30 days. Refresher training is triggered automatically after any reported phishing click, regardless of how minor the incident.

Phishing Simulation:
Phishing simulations run quarterly on a random sample of inboxes. Simulations are non-punitive and reported only at team level, never used for individual performance management. Repeated failures at team level trigger a targeted session rather than discipline.

What to Do When You Click:
Report it immediately to security@novatech.in. A fast report is always treated as a win, even if you submitted credentials. Speed of reporting matters far more than whether you made the mistake. Never attempt to assess or clean up the damage yourself.

Reporting Success:
Employees who report a real phishing email receive a thank-you from the security team. Reports of genuine phishing average 11 per week, and the fastest report in the last quarter was 4 minutes from delivery.

Recognising Phishing:
Treat urgency, authority claims, misspelled sender domains, unexpected attachments, and requests for credentials or payment details as red flags. Verify any payment or credential request through a second channel before acting. Hover over links to inspect the true destination before clicking.

SECTION 10: SECURE DEVELOPMENT AND PIPELINE SECURITY
============================================================

CI Pipeline Security:
Every build runs with short-lived credentials injected at runtime. Long-lived tokens are never stored in the pipeline configuration. Pipeline configuration lives in the infrastructure repository and all changes require review by a platform engineer.

Secret Scanning:
Repositories are scanned for committed secrets on every push. A detected secret blocks the push immediately rather than alerting after the fact. Any committed secret must be rotated before the commit is reverted, because the secret is assumed compromised once pushed.

Production Access From CI:
Production deployment uses short-lived, job-scoped credentials issued to a single pipeline run. No long-lived production key exists anywhere. Deployment to production requires an approved change request, matching the release process defined in engineering standards.

Supply Chain:
Dependencies are scanned for known vulnerabilities on every build. Critical severity vulnerabilities must be remediated within 7 days and High within 30 days. Base container images are rebuilt weekly. Dependencies with copyleft licences require legal approval.

Container Security:
Containers run as a non-root user with a read-only root filesystem where possible. No container image may embed secrets or credentials. Images are scanned before deployment, and critical image findings block release.

============================================================
SECTION 11: DATA RETENTION AND LEGAL HOLD
============================================================

Retention Periods:
Operational business data is retained for 7 years. Security logs and authentication logs are retained for 12 months. Access reviews and audit evidence are retained for 3 years. Employee records follow the HR retention schedule of 8 years post-separation.

Deletion on Separation:
Employee personal data is deleted from systems within 30 days of separation, except where retention is required by law or an active legal hold. Backups containing separated employee data are aged out through the standard backup rotation cycle.

Legal Hold:
A legal hold suspends all deletion for a defined scope of data. Holds are issued only by Legal, in writing, and are never removed verbally. Recipients must preserve the specified data indefinitely until Legal confirms in writing that the hold is released. Deleting data under hold is a disciplinary matter.

Backups and Retention:
Deleting data from the primary system does not remove it from backups. Backup retention is 35 days, after which deleted data ages out. Data subject deletion requests are therefore fulfilled within 30 days but complete eradication from backups takes up to 35 days.

============================================================
SECTION 12: BUSINESS CONTINUITY AND DISASTER RECOVERY
============================================================

Recovery Objectives:
Tier 1 customer-facing systems have a Recovery Time Objective (RTO) of 1 hour and a Recovery Point Objective (RPO) of 15 minutes. Tier 2 internal systems have an RTO of 4 hours and an RPO of 1 hour.

Backup Policy:
Production databases are backed up every 6 hours with a daily snapshot retained for 35 days and monthly snapshots retained for 12 months. Backups are encrypted with keys stored separately from the backups themselves. Backup restoration is tested quarterly by a named engineer who did not perform the backup.

Redundancy:
Production traffic is served from at least 2 availability zones. A single availability zone failure must not result in customer-visible downtime. Multi-region failover is maintained for Tier 1 systems, with a documented failover runbook exercised twice a year.

Degraded Mode:
If recovery from an outage takes longer than the RTO, teams must consider a documented degraded mode — such as read-only access or disabling a non-critical feature — rather than leaving customers with a broken interface. Degraded mode must be declared by the incident commander and communicated on the status page.

============================================================
SECTION 13: AI AND LARGE LANGUAGE MODEL SECURITY
============================================================

Approved AI Tools:
The company-approved external AI tools are ChatGPT Team and Claude for Enterprise, both under company-managed accounts. Personal accounts of AI tools are not approved for work use because they lack contractual data protection terms.

Data That Must Never Be Entered:
Customer data, employee personal data, salaries, credentials, API keys, source code from Restricted systems, and any data classified Confidential or above must never be entered into an external AI tool. This applies to prompts, file uploads, and screenshots. There is no exception for small amounts of data.

Approved Internal Assistant:
For sensitive queries, use the company-approved internal AI assistant, which runs entirely on company-managed infrastructure with retention disabled. When in doubt about whether a query is sensitive, use the internal assistant — it is never penalised to use it.

Code Generated by AI:
Code generated by an AI tool must be reviewed with the same rigor as human-written code, including dependency and security review. Developers are accountable for generated code as if they had written it. AI-generated code is not exempt from the review and testing requirements in engineering standards.

Prompt Injection Awareness:
Never let AI tools read untrusted content such as customer support tickets or inbound email and then act on the instructions inside it. Content retrieved by an AI tool is data, not instruction. Treat any instruction found inside retrieved content as untrusted input and escalate if it appears hostile.
